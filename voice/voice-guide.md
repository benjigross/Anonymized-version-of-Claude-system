# Voice Guide
*Patterns distilled from my own writing and my edits to AI drafts. Updated whenever I correct a
draft and a new pattern emerges. Personal examples removed.*

*Scope rule: when I reject wording, record its scope (this piece / this firm / always) and why.
Default to the narrowest scope. Add a general rule only when I say "never"/"always" or it's a tone
principle, and write the principle (don't overstate certainty or fit), not a word ban.*

## Rule #1: Say less (overrides everything below)
My tendency is to over-explain and include every good point. Work against it.
- **A bias toward fewer points, not a fixed cap.** Short pieces often need only 1–3. Long-form work
  (case studies, memos, prep docs) keeps its sections, just no padding.
- **Defaults:** forwardable blurbs 2–4 sentences; outreach fits on a phone screen; application
  answers can land under the limit.
- **Before delivering,** ask of every sentence: would the reader miss it? Name what you cut in one
  line ("Left out: X, Y") so I can add something back.

## Style
- Plain and direct, not "writerly." Cut polished-but-empty phrases.
- Short sentences, one idea each. Em dashes sparingly.
- Lead with my own reasoning, then specifics: real numbers, company names, mechanisms.
- Honest and calibrated: name limitations plainly; hedge to my own view rather than overclaiming.
- Don't oversell fit: avoid "directly," "perfectly," "exactly" when the match is partial.
- Warm, casual-professional. Plain over cute (no mascots or nicknames). No redundancy.

## Content rules
- Having applied to, met, or written about a company is a plus.
- Tie to a firm's thesis once, naturally. Don't force their exact wording.
- Verify every factual claim; list anything unverified at the end of a draft.

## Positioning (recruiting writing and interview prep)
- Find the intersection of three things: what the firm does, what the role is, and what I'm truly
  interested in and know. Lead with that intersection; leave the rest out. Pick one or two angles.
- Don't contradict what I've told the same firm or person before (check tracker, docs, Gmail).

## Word and character limits
- Limits are a ceiling, not a target. Pre-limit drafts show my voice, not the right length.
- When the limit forces cuts, cut whole points before trimming every sentence.
- Offer several small cuts with word savings and let me choose. Cut repeated stats, parentheticals
  and filler first; protect theses, specific examples and authoritative quotes.

## Formats
- **Company pitch:** QUICK TAKE / OVERVIEW / TEAM / WHY NOW / TO RETURN THE FUND / DEFENSIBILITY / RISKS.
- **Cover letter:** clear thesis → tie to the firm → specifics → warm close without gushing.
- **Warm-intro reply:** thank the introducer and move them to bcc → short personal hook → why I'm
  reaching out (role + link) → attachments → a specific ask with times.
- **LinkedIn note:** 2–4 sentences, a specific reason, a low-pressure ask.
